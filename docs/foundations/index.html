Foundations of Anthroposophy

One clear route from ordinary experience to the central concepts, then a choice of deeper studies.

Study in order. Allow roughly 15–25 minutes for each core lesson and its question; full source readings are optional and take additional time. This is an introduction to understanding the subject, not a complete spiritual training.

Begin Lesson 1 →
  1. 01 · What are we studying?

    Understand anthroposophy as a proposed path of knowledge, and distinguish a foundation course from a complete spiritual training.

  2. 02 · Observation, interpretation and knowledge

    Distinguish what you encounter from the explanation you form.

  3. 03 · Body, soul and spirit: three relationships to the world

    Explain the distinction using one ordinary encounter.

  4. 04 · Thinking, feeling and willing: activities, not another list of bodies

    Explain the distinction using one ordinary encounter.

  5. 05 · Matter and living organization

    Understand the physical and etheric members before studying sensation.

  6. 06 · Inward experience and the I

    Complete the fourfold account by distinguishing sensation from individual selfhood.

  7. 07 · The threefold bodily organization

    Distinguish a functional account of bodily life from body, soul and spirit.

  8. 08 · How the accounts relate without becoming interchangeable

    Compare organizing questions before comparing lists.

  9. 09 · How inner life becomes differentiated

    Follow the movement from sensation to reflection and conscious judgment.

  10. 10 · Individuality and spiritual development

    Distinguish the I, consciousness soul and spirit-self.

  11. 11 · Memory, capacity and biography

    Understand what an experience can leave behind.

  12. 12 · Freedom and responsible action

    Ask how a motive becomes your own considered reason for acting.

  13. 13 · Reincarnation: the proposed continuity of individuality

    Reconstruct the proposal without treating analogy as proof.

  14. 14 · Karma: action, consequence and destiny

    Distinguish responsibility from a fixed or punitive account of fate.

  15. 15 · What Steiner means by the soul world

    Distinguish a claimed realm of soul reality from an everyday mood.

  16. 16 · What Steiner means by the spiritual world

    Understand the meaning of archetype before studying detailed cosmology.

  17. 17 · The path of knowledge

    Distinguish studying spiritual concepts from the capacities Steiner describes.

  18. 18 · Bring the relationships together

    Explain the foundation and choose a purposeful next study.
