Individuality and spiritual development

Distinguish the I, consciousness soul and spirit-self.

Builds on: How inner life becomes differentiated.

Understand the relationship

The word “I” has a special position: when we use it about ourselves, it refers from within our own standpoint. Steiner uses this as an entrance to spiritual individuality. The ability to pronounce the word, however, is not the same as understanding the full concept developed in the chapter.

The I is not simply selfishness. It is the center through which, in this account, the person relates soul life to spirit and participates in transformation. The consciousness soul is described at the boundary of soul and spirit; spirit self names the spiritual content received and made one’s own. Closely connected does not mean interchangeable.

The book’s technical use of intuition concerns direct spiritual knowing. It should not be reduced to a hunch or a strong feeling of certainty. The addenda qualify the language surrounding individuality and knowing. To understand the passage, explain what role each term plays before deciding whether you accept the proposed spiritual account.
