The path of knowledge

Distinguish studying spiritual concepts from the capacities Steiner describes.

Builds on: What Steiner means by the spiritual world.

Understand the relationship

Understanding a spiritual account and claiming to perceive its subject are different achievements. Reading this course develops conceptual understanding. Steiner’s path of knowledge concerns a further transformation of capacities, accompanied by work on attention, judgment and conduct.

Imagination, Inspiration and Intuition name forms of spiritual cognition in this context. Imagination concerns image-like spiritual perception; Inspiration concerns apprehending spiritual relationships and meaning beyond the images; Intuition concerns participation in the being known. They do not simply mean inventiveness, enthusiasm and a hunch.

These brief definitions orient the next book study; they are not instructions for producing the experiences. Nor should every use of intuition elsewhere be assigned this meaning automatically. Read the term in the argument of the particular work.

An introductory practice can be modest: describe an object carefully, notice an assumption, and reconsider it. Such work develops habits relevant to study. Assess the practice by the quality of attention and judgment it supports, without treating a feeling of certainty as certification of spiritual knowledge.
