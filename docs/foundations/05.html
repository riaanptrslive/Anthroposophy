Matter and living organization

Understand the physical and etheric members before studying sensation.

Builds on: Thinking, feeling and willing: activities, not another list of bodies.

Understand the relationship

The fourfold account asks: what must an account of a living, experiencing, self-conscious human being include? Steiner distinguishes material existence, living formation, inward experience and individual selfhood. He names the corresponding members physical body, etheric or life body, astral body, and I. Here “member” means a distinguishable aspect of the whole.

Physical body: what gives the human being material existence? This is the body considered in relation to physical substances and forces. A living body is physical too; “physical” does not mean dead. What remains to be explained is the organization of that material as a living whole.

Etheric or life body: what maintains and develops a living form? Steiner proposes a formative organization active in growth, maintenance and reproduction. “Etheric” names this proposed supersensible organization; it is not a chemical substance or the ether once proposed in physics. Growing leaves illustrate the problem of living form; observing them does not by itself demonstrate his explanation.

Why a living form is more than an inventory of materials

An inventory identifies the materials present at a moment. A living-form description follows an organization through change: a plant takes up materials, produces new leaves and maintains a recognizable pattern while its contents change. Steiner introduces the etheric or life body to explain this continuity of formative activity. It is not another visible substance poured into the physical body, and it is not simply a second name for the observed growth.

Compare a pile of plant material with the growing plant from which it came. Similar materials do not amount to the same organized activity. This makes the explanatory question clear. Steiner’s answer adds a supersensible organization; the observed contrast does not by itself select that answer over every other explanation. His later distinction of sensation adds another question: a plant’s development and an animal’s inward pleasure or pain are not the same kind of description. The progression is material embodiment, living organization, then sentient experience.
