The twenties: meeting the world

Explain exploration, feedback and emerging responsibility.

Understand → read closely → apply → revise

Understand the chapter

The key ideas, explained

Early adulthood is a gradual transition in the book. A person begins building a place in the world through work, friendship, a home or other commitments. External recognition can strongly affect the developing sense of self.

Lievegoed connects this period with the sentient soul and vital-mental strivings: experiences are felt vividly and help form judgment. Variety matters because the person is still discovering how ideals, abilities and the actual environment fit together.

Responsibility makes exploration consequential. Commitments bring other people’s needs into view. In relationships, the author asks for recognition of the other individual beyond attraction. Read that principle separately from his assumption that marriage and a conventional household are the usual route.

A passage to read closely

Adolescence slides gradually into adulthood until the characteristics of the latter predominate.

Bernard Lievegoed · Phases · Book chapter 2.3 · PDF capture page 41.

Read the wording

“Gradually” makes a transition more important than a birthday. Different areas of life may change at different speeds.

A worked example

Rui interprets criticism of his first presentation as proof he chose the wrong profession. A mentor separates the task feedback from that sweeping conclusion.

The experience can teach a skill without deciding Rui’s entire worth or future. This illustrates the relationship between outer feedback and inner stability.

Three key takeaways

  • Adulthood emerges unevenly and gradually.
  • Feedback can teach without defining identity.
  • Commitment includes another person’s reality.
