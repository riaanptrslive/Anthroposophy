The early fifties: experience becomes contribution

Contrast authority based on status with authority that develops others.

Understand → read closely → apply → revise

Understand the chapter

The key ideas, explained

In the early fifties, Lievegoed contrasts two possibilities. One person protects status against younger colleagues; another uses accumulated experience to see a wider field and support others. The contrast concerns the use of experience, not simply its quantity.

His “eminent leader” can be a foreman, teacher or manager. Broader judgment connects immediate decisions with their larger consequences. Mentoring becomes a way to give experience a future beyond one’s own performance.

Distance from everyday problems should mean perspective, not indifference. The author also describes renewed artistic and community interests. These themes can be read beyond the gender-specific work and family roles used in his examples.

A passage to read closely

Life becomes more interesting, the distance to little everyday problems increases.

Bernard Lievegoed · Phases · Book chapter 2.7 · PDF capture page 57.

Read the wording

Read “distance” with the surrounding concern for wider significance. It names perspective rather than permission to neglect people.

A worked example

An experienced craftsperson lets an apprentice try a different method, then discusses the result instead of taking the tool away.

Experience supports another person’s learning. Authority remains present through attention and judgment.

Three key takeaways

  • Experience can widen perspective.
  • Mentoring gives knowledge a future.
  • Generosity includes allowing others to act.
