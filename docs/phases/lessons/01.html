Growth, development and the three dimensions

Distinguish quantitative growth from a change in the organization of life.

Understand → read closely → apply → revise

Understand the chapter

The key ideas, explained

Chapter 1 separates change, growth and development. Change is the broadest term. Growth adds quantity; development reorganizes a whole. Differentiation creates distinct functions, while integration brings them into a new relationship. A crisis may mark the point where the old arrangement no longer works.

Lievegoed sketches bodily maturation, equilibrium and decline; psychological outward engagement, balance and inwardness; and spiritual creativity, fulfillment and wisdom. These are related perspectives. Reduced physical capacity does not, in his account, require reduced meaning or judgment.

He contrasts recurring desire and satisfaction with an aim that can be fulfilled. Creativity turns the mind towards action in the world; wisdom involves receptive understanding. His “active peace” combines inward composure with engagement, rather than withdrawal from responsibility.

A passage to read closely

Creativity is activity by the mind in the world.

Bernard Lievegoed · Phases · Book chapter 1.1–1.3 · PDF capture page 16.

Read the wording

“In the world” matters: creativity here includes ordinary work and social action, not only artistic talent.

A worked example

A team adds more meetings but still makes every decision through one person. Later it shares responsibility and changes how decisions are made.

More meetings are quantitative change. The new relationship between roles illustrates structural development. Neither change is automatically an improvement; its effects need examining.

Three key takeaways

  • More is different from differently organized.
  • The three dimensions need not rise and fall together.
  • Creativity can appear in everyday responsibility.

Check your understanding

Is every crisis development?

Show an explained answer

No. A crisis may open a transition, but integration and a new organization are not guaranteed.

How do desire and aim differ here?

Show an explained answer

A satisfied desire can recur; an aim can give direction and culminate in fulfillment.

Must wisdom mean inactivity?

Show an explained answer

No. Receptivity can inform engaged, thoughtful action.
