The organizational phase: structure without rigidity

Recognize the achievement and risk of consolidating a way of life.

Understand → read closely → apply → revise

Understand the chapter

The key ideas, explained

Around the late twenties and early thirties, Lievegoed describes a stronger wish to organize. Planning, continuity and persistence turn experience into a workable structure. The person wants to demonstrate competence and carry responsibility over time.

He relates this to the intellectual soul and objective strivings. Practical judgment can become less dependent on immediate feeling. Work and relationships develop routines that support complex commitments.

The same strength has a risk: treating the successful arrangement as the only possible one. Acquaintances may become useful contacts and disagreement an inconvenience. Development asks whether the structure still serves people and the original purpose.

A structure should continue to serve its purpose

Organization turns repeated effort into something dependable. A schedule, an agreed division of work or a method for keeping promises reduces the need to begin again each day. In Lievegoed’s account, this can express a growing capacity to carry a purpose through changing moods. Structure is therefore an achievement before it becomes a possible limitation.

The limitation appears when the arrangement becomes more important than the purpose it served. Imagine a team retaining a procedure that no longer helps its members because it once worked well. The developmental question is not whether all routine is bad, but whether a person can reassess a successful routine. Explain what the structure makes possible, what it now obstructs and what revision would preserve its useful work. This makes the contrast between competence and rigidity concrete without assigning it to everyone of a particular age.

Reading connection: Phases · Chapter 2 · organizational phase.

A passage to read closely

The danger of a hardening of attitudes and of egotism is now particularly great.

Bernard Lievegoed · Phases · Book chapter 2.4 · PDF capture page 46.

Read the wording

“Danger” identifies a possible distortion of competence, not a verdict on everyone in their thirties.

A worked example

A coordinator’s reliable schedule helps everyone work. When a colleague suggests an improvement, she refuses to consider it because the schedule is hers.

The structure remains valuable; ownership has become entangled with identity. Testing the proposal preserves competence while reopening learning.

Three key takeaways

  • Organization can express growing responsibility.
  • A successful method can harden into an identity.
  • Review what the structure serves.
