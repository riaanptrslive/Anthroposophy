Explore the chapter’s core ideas

Understand the idea

The three soul members describe different ways of taking up experience. The sentient soul is the inward life of sensation, attraction, aversion and desire. The intellectual or mind soul works this life through in thought. Thinking at this level can serve appetite, convenience and practical needs; using intelligence does not automatically mean seeking truth independently of oneself.

The consciousness soul introduces that further orientation. Steiner distinguishes the validity of a truth from the pleasure a person feels in knowing it. He also distinguishes truth from a changing human opinion about what is true. Discovering that yesterday’s belief was mistaken does not, in his argument, show that truth is whatever someone happens to believe today.

The sequence is therefore not three boxes for emotion, intelligence and self-awareness. It follows a development from experienced content, through thought working on experience, toward an acknowledged validity beyond personal inclination. The same person can participate in all these activities. Use this sequence to explain why a clever way of satisfying a desire and a reasoned correction of one’s own belief are different achievements.

Keep this idea

A correction can be true even when it is unpleasant to accept.

From immediate response to a judgment that can be examined

The sentient soul concerns the inward reception of sensation and desire. The intellectual or mind soul brings experience into thought and relationship. The consciousness soul emphasizes a relation to truth that is not determined merely by personal sympathy or advantage. These are distinctions within inner life, not three kinds of people and not a ranking of someone’s worth.

Suppose a colleague corrects a date you have written. The immediate sting belongs to your experienced response. Considering what the colleague meant and comparing the two dates engages reflective activity. Checking the calendar and accepting the result even when it is unwelcome illustrates the movement toward a judgment that is not settled by liking. All these moments may overlap. The example helps explain the distinctions; it does not assign a soul category to the person or imply that feeling must disappear before truth becomes accessible.

What this passage means

These names mark a development from experiencing impressions to working with them in thought and relating to truth beyond personal preference. Read the three as connected functions of soul life.
