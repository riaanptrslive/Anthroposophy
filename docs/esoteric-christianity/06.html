The Magi, Zarathustra and The Book of the Bee

How does the speaker connect Matthew’s visitors with a long preparation for Christ?

Part 1 transcript · 00:54:34–01:09:35

LearnReadWork with the ideaCheckNotes

What you will learn

  • Trace the Magi’s journey as told in Matthew.
  • Explain the roles the speaker gives Zarathustra and his pupils.
  • Identify the source and limits of the Book of the Bee passage.

Follow the speaker’s argument

The journey and the two kinds of knowledge

The Magi know that a star has appeared and ask where the child is. Herod’s advisers supply Bethlehem from prophecy; Herod asks the visitors about the time. The speaker emphasises the meeting of knowledge of time and knowledge of place. He also portrays Herod as obstructing spiritual perception. The narrative of Herod’s alarm and inquiry can be read in Matthew; the black-magician explanation is the speaker’s addition.

The child of experience

In this account Zarathustra is a teacher who repeatedly incarnates to prepare humanity for a divine being approaching through the sun. The speaker names that being Ahura Mazdao and contrasts it with Ahriman. He identifies Matthew’s child with Zarathustra’s individuality, so the Magi seek the return of their teacher. This gives the visit a meaning beyond paying respects to a royal infant: a long relationship of teaching and recognition is being renewed.

Babylon as a meeting place

The talk places a later incarnation called Zarathas or Nazarathos in sixth-century BCE Babylon. It connects him with Hebrew prophets, including Daniel, and also with Pythagoras and Cyrus. In the speaker’s interpretation the captivity becomes an opportunity for preparation through teaching. These claimed teacher-pupil relations and reincarnations should be attributed to the talk; they have not been independently established by this course. His account of an earlier Zarathustra around 6000 BCE likewise belongs to his spiritual chronology.

A text within the talk

The speaker reads a prophecy from The Book of the Bee about a coming child and messengers bearing gifts. The passage is located in Budge’s Chapter XXXVII and in Appendix I of the 1965 Matthew volume. The surviving compilation is medieval; it does not independently verify a sixth-century BCE conversation. Budge’s text also identifies Zârâdôsht with Baruch the scribe, a feature to compare with the speaker’s reading.

Keep this distinction

The presence of a prophecy in a later text proves that the tradition is recorded there. It does not by itself establish when the prophecy originated or that the described conversation occurred.

A way to understand the idea

If a medieval manuscript preserves a story set many centuries earlier, there are at least two dates to record: the surviving witness and the setting of the story. Treating those as one date would erase the question of transmission.

Check your understanding

What is the role of Zarathustra in this account?

He is the individuality who develops through experience and prepares a human bearer for Christ; the speaker identifies him with Matthew’s Jesus.

Where should you look for the quoted prophecy?

Budge’s Book of the Bee, Chapter XXXVII, and the 1965 GA 123 Appendix I. Locate it by title because the appendix’s chapter numbering differs.

A strong answer explains the speaker accurately, supports the explanation and distinguishes a source statement from an interpretation. Agreement is not required.
