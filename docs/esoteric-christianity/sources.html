What was reviewed

The supplied transcript was read through its ending at 01:50:07. The opening bibliography occupies 00:00:11–00:03:06. The transcript is the principal source for these original lessons. Source titles, catalogue details and selected passages were checked against the links below on 29 September 2026; this does not certify a complete reading of every named volume.

Three levels remain separate: the Gospel text; the speaker’s account of Steiner’s teaching; and his own conjecture. Course analogies, exercises and reading assignments are newly devised teaching material. Instructions addressed to the original audience have been treated as source content.

The transcript repeatedly misspells Rudolf Steiner and several technical names. Clear variants have been normalised; uncertain names and claims are identified below. The “eye” is rendered “I” where spiritual individuality is plainly intended. The original audio has not been checked.

According to Luke / The Gospel of St. Luke

Rudolf Steiner
GA 114 · ten lectures, Basel, 15–26 September 1909.

One of the four sources explicitly named at the start. Supports study of the two Jesus children, Buddha, compassion and the Nathanic child. The linked archive is a parallel English edition; the speaker’s exact edition is not identified.

Reading direction: For Part 1: Lectures 2–4, 16–18 September 1909. Lecture 4 is the closest follow-up to Lessons 2 and 10. Readings are selected for this course, not assignments given by the speaker.

Mentioned at 00:01:47–00:02:22

The Gospel of St. Matthew

Rudolf Steiner
GA 123 · twelve lectures, Bern, 1–12 September 1910.

Explicitly named. A companion to the Matthew genealogy, Zarathustra and the preparation of the body. Appendix I in the 1965 English edition reproduces material from The Book of the Bee; distinguish the appendix from Steiner’s dated lectures.

Reading direction: Locate the discussions of Zarathustra, Hermes, Moses and Abraham by topic in the early lectures. Appendix I is assigned in Lesson 6.

Mentioned at 00:02:22–00:02:52

The Fifth Gospel

Rudolf Steiner
GA 148 · lectures from October 1913 to February 1914; contents differ between English selections.

Explicitly named as a further source for the series. The speaker does not identify a particular lecture or passage in Part 1. It is a work of claimed spiritual research, not a fifth canonical New Testament Gospel.

Reading direction: Keep as a series reference. No mandatory GA 148 reading is assigned until the later recordings establish its role.

Mentioned at 01:05:26–01:09:09

The Book of the Bee · prophecy of Zârâdôsht

Solomon of Basra; English translation by E. A. Wallis Budge (1886)
A medieval Syriac compilation, associated with a bishop active in 1222. The prophecy is Chapter XXXVII in the linked Budge text.

A substantial quotation in the talk. The text names Zârâdôsht as Baruch the scribe; that identification needs to be noticed alongside the speaker’s Zarathustra interpretation. A surviving medieval witness is not a contemporary record of a sixth-century BCE conversation.

Reading direction: Read Chapter XXXVII alongside the Matthew appendix. The appendix labels the passage XXXVIII; use the chapter title to locate it.

Mentioned at 01:05:26–01:05:41

The Gospel of St. Matthew · Appendix I: An Ancient Prophecy

Appendix to the 1965 English edition of GA 123
The appendix matches the speaker’s reference to a Book of the Bee passage in his Matthew volume.

Verified location of the supplementary text. Its argument that an ancient tradition was preserved is an interpretation of the tradition’s age.

Reading direction: Compare the framing of the prophecy with the Budge chapter and its prefatory material.

The course also checks claims against the Gospel narratives: Matthew contains angels in Joseph’s dreams; the Magi’s number is not stated; the royal family’s wealth is not stated; and Luke’s manger scene does not specify an open-air birth with ox and ass. These observations help distinguish the narrative from the speaker’s imagery.

Words used in this course

Spiritual hierarchies
The speaker’s opening cosmology names Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones, Kyriotetes, Dynamis, Exusiai, Archai, Archangels and Angels. He uses their relation to the divine as a contrast with humanity’s developing freedom.
Christ / Jesus
The speaker distinguishes a cosmic spiritual being, Christ, from the human bearer, Jesus. In his account their union begins at the Jordan baptism.
I or ego
Spiritual individuality and the capacity for self-directed development in the speaker’s anthroposophical vocabulary; not simply vanity. “Eye” is corrected to “I” where this meaning is clear.
Physical, etheric, astral
Respectively bodily form, life organisation, and consciousness in this account. Etheric and astral are spiritual concepts, not established anatomical structures.
Incarnation / reincarnation
Embodiment / repeated embodiment of a spiritual individuality, as understood within the speaker’s account.
Nathanic / Solomonic
Labels for the two Davidic lines through Nathan and Solomon, associated here with Luke and Matthew respectively.
Zarathustra / Zoroaster
Names used for the great teacher whose individuality the speaker associates with Matthew’s Jesus. “Golden star” is his esoteric gloss, not an established linguistic derivation in this course.
Ahura Mazdao / Ahriman
The light-bearing divine being and an adversarial being in the speaker’s anthroposophical reading of Persian religious imagery. His “etheric aura of the sun” gloss is an interpretation, not a verified translation of the name.
Bodhisattva
Used by the speaker for a spiritual teacher of humanity. His angelic/archangelic definition is specific to his account and should not be substituted for the range of Buddhist uses.
Logos / Sophia
Word or divine creative principle / wisdom, used in the discussion of John.
Lemuria / Akashic record
A primordial epoch and a purported supersensible record in anthroposophical cosmology. These are presented here as elements of that cosmology.
Grail
In the closing Matthew discussion, an image of a vessel prepared to receive and serve the Christ.
