About the edition and source coverage

Practical Training in Thought · Rudolf Steiner

Parallel primary lecture online, 18 January 1909, GA 108, George Adams translation. Retained numbered local paragraph extracts are German despite English-oriented filenames; do not quote them as an English transcription.

This curriculum uses the existing chapter analyses. It does not certify a new complete reading or fill a missing source with a different edition without attribution.

LecturePractical Training in Thought · Karlsruhe, 18 January 190910 lessons

  1. Habit and practicality · thinking fitted to its object

    Habit or a reason?

    Distinguish a familiar routine from a reason that can be examined.

    Reading: Paragraphs 1–8

  2. Thought in the world · the spiritual premise of inquiry

    An attitude of inquiry

    Distinguish spiritual creative activity from the human thinking that understands it, and connect this idea to careful observation.

    Reading: Paragraphs 9–13

  3. Observation and concept · disciplined description

    Observe before explaining

    Distinguish percept, mental picture and concept; understand where the astral body enters Steiner’s interpretation.

    Reading: GA 108 §§ 14–18; GA 4, chapter VI §§ 4–6, in the linked English translations.

  4. Successive states · continuity and change

    Follow a change

    Compare successive observations without filling gaps with certainty.

    Reading: Paragraphs 14–18

  5. Anticipation · correction by events

    Make a prediction you can check

    Use an outcome to revise an expectation.

    Reading: Paragraphs 19–21

  6. Reconstruction · evidence and imagined causes

    Investigate how something happened

    Propose an earlier cause, seek evidence and revise your explanation.

    Reading: Paragraphs 22–26

  7. Self-directed attention · chosen thought

    Choose and sustain a line of thought

    Deliberately choose a subject outside habitual concerns; distinguish this exercise from detailed recollection.

    Reading: Adams English translation, § 28: choice of subject and transition to the memory exercise.

  8. Memory · imaginative completion and checking

    Build and check a mental picture

    Reconstruct details of an encounter and distinguish what you remember, do not know and imagine.

    Reading: Adams English translation, end of § 28 and §§ 29–32: memory and imaginative completion.

  9. Suspended judgment · alternatives and causal inference

    Compare, pause and decide

    Examine alternative actions, set the question aside and return with reasons for a provisional choice.

    Reading: Paragraphs 33–36; compare the cause-and-effect example in § 38.

  10. Cause and effect · practical judgment

    Review how your thinking changed

    Use earlier notes to assess a specific change and choose further practice.

    Reading: Paragraphs 38–48 for the conclusion; revisit §§ 1–36 for the exercises.

A rhythm you can return to

Read → understand → practise → return and revise. Allow about 15–25 minutes for a lesson, then three short practice sessions on separate occasions. These are planning estimates; source reading takes additional time. Work at your own pace.

Keep dates, observations and revisions in the lesson notebook. The final lesson returns to your earlier entries. Saving is optional and stays in this browser; export a copy for safekeeping.

Which exercise fits your question?

Follow the lessons in order on your first reading. Afterwards use this map to revisit a specific difficulty. Observing, predicting, investigating a cause, directing attention, remembering and deciding are related tasks with different aims.
