Reversing the order changes your relation to the day

The backward review asks for deliberate reconstruction instead of letting the strongest event pull memory along its usual associations. Begin with a later event and move toward earlier ones, preserving the distinction between what you remember and what you infer. The change of direction is part of the exercise, not a test of how much detail can be recovered.

Observe yourself as one participant in the scene. What did you say, what did another person do, and what interpretation followed? This can make a familiar response visible without turning the review into self-accusation or an imaginary correction of the past. A gap may remain a gap. The purpose is a more conscious relation to the sequence and to your participation in it.

What this passage means

This related self-observation passage asks for distance from one’s earlier reactions. It does not itself specify reverse order. The backward review taught below adds the evening-to-morning sequence from the assigned meditation material; distinguish the direction of recall from the observing attitude.

The focus of this lesson

Reconstruct selected events in reverse order and distinguish self-observation from self-condemnation.

Why this comes here

The verse directs attention to chosen content; the review brings deliberate attention to one’s own actions. It links inner practice with responsibility.

What the source is teaching

Steiner asks for an evening look back from the present toward the morning. Different passages emphasize learning from important experiences, forming clear images of small events, and viewing oneself with the distance used toward another person. Reverse order interrupts the automatic replay of the day and requires active reconstruction.

The book also relates this activity to supersensible perception and, in a further passage, to the development of love as a cognitive capacity. These are the larger spiritual purposes Steiner proposes. A useful correction in tomorrow’s conversation is a meaningful practical result, but does not itself establish those further claims.

How to work with it

Historical directions are identified above. The guided steps below are our teaching arrangement of the selected practice.

  1. Before sleep, choose a short manageable portion of the day. Our introductory adaptation is three events rather than the entire day.
  2. Picture the latest event first, then the preceding one, then the earlier one. Keep the events accurate while reversing their order.
  3. Include your own action in each scene. Distinguish what you remember clearly from details you are supplying; do not invent completeness.
  4. Ask what can be learned and name one possible repair or adjustment. Finish the review rather than repeatedly replaying a painful scene. The source emphasizes clarity without bodily strain.

Check the connection

Why go backward rather than just recount the day?

Read an explanation

Reverse order makes the sequence a deliberate inner activity rather than a habitual replay. Accurate reconstruction and a little distance make room for learning; the purpose is not an exhaustive inventory of faults.
