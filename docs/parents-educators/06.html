In a learning group

Learners construct a small shelter or complete a drawing. Their different decisions become material for conversation about the process.

Understand the idea

The Colour course explains Steiner’s proposal that form can arise through colour relationships. Koepke’s curriculum discussion connects practical work with questions of shelter, cultivation and belonging. These are distinct source contexts, brought together here through a shared interest in meaningful activity.

The adult should be able to explain what the activity helps someone notice or understand. A beautiful product alone does not reveal the learning process. Leave room for discovery, suitable materials and the learner’s account of a decision.

Apply the chapter’s concepts

You and a child make two colour studies: one begins with an outline, the other with areas of colour that suggest a form.

Which of the concepts above helps explain this situation? Keep the illustration distinct from the author’s wider claims.

Work with the idea

Prepare one brief making, drawing or planting activity. State its learning purpose, accessible materials and one question about the process. Participate or use a fictional plan.

Check your understanding

How can two different finished pictures demonstrate the same learning aim?

Compare with explained feedback

Both learners may explain how a colour relationship influenced a decision. The aim is to notice and articulate that process, rather than reproduce one picture. Ask for a specific decision and its reason.

When the situation changes

The planned materials are unavailable. Find another medium and explain which aspect of the learning aim it preserves.

Record one decision discovered during the activity and one change you would make to the invitation.
