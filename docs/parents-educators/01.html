In a learning group

A learner does not begin. The paper is blank, but that alone does not explain whether the instructions, materials or task are the difficulty.

Understand the idea

Observation and thinking cooperate: you notice, distinguish and connect. The important question is whether the connection you propose is supported. “The page is blank” describes something available to inspection; “the learner does not care” adds an account of their motive.

Practical Thinking develops this attention to the actual situation. The linked meditation lesson brings the adult’s own actions into review. Its spiritual context is wider than this activity; here we use a short observation record to make our first assumptions available for reconsideration.

Apply the chapter’s concepts

A child leaves a drawing unfinished. You think, “They give up too easily.” You have not yet asked what happened.

Which of the concepts above helps explain this situation? Keep the illustration distinct from the author’s wider claims.

Work with the idea

Record one ordinary encounter in two columns: what was said or done, and the explanation you formed. Include your own words. Use the fictional scene if you prefer.

Check your understanding

What would you ask or observe before deciding why the drawing was left unfinished?

Compare with explained feedback

Ask what the child was trying to do and where they stopped. Check the available materials and time. Several explanations could fit a blank page; a useful question helps distinguish them.

When the situation changes

Now the child says the pencil broke and the sharpener was out of reach. Which part of your first explanation needs revision?

On another occasion, compare your first interpretation with what you learned by asking. Retain the uncertainty where you still lack information.
