Final project: one meal, many relationships

Create a source-supported meal portfolio that connects farming, food, cooking and shared life.

Understand → read closely → apply → revise

Understand the chapter

The key ideas, explained

The final project follows one meal through the whole book. Begin with its ingredients and cultivation; move through plant form, processing and preparation; end with the people, setting and consequences. A small meal can reveal many relationships when its choices are explained clearly.

Use at least five chapters across all four parts. Chapter 2 can support a farming question; Chapter 5 a distinction about nourishment; Chapters 7–18 an ingredient comparison; Chapter 19 a cooking plan; Chapter 20 an ethical reflection. Explain the connection to each cited passage rather than collecting decorative quotations.

Separate Cook’s ideas, what you observed, what current evidence supports and what remains unresolved. This is how the course’s close-reading method becomes independent judgment. You can complete the project as a plan; buying special ingredients or changing your diet is unnecessary.

Build a chain from understanding to a feasible action

Cook ends by asking whether our way of obtaining and preparing food supports our ability to turn intentions into action. Her spiritual explanation concerns the vitality of food and the relation between thinking and will. The practical challenge can be examined without assuming that explanation is established: what would change in a real meal if its participants took cultivation, preparation and shared responsibility seriously?

Choose one manageable decision and trace its consequences. For example, planning a seasonal shared dish connects a grower, an ingredient, a method, a budget and people’s needs. Explain one link at a time and identify a tradeoff. A portfolio is stronger when it shows why a choice fits these conditions than when it collects approving phrases from many chapters. Finish with a review question: what would you observe after trying the plan, and what result would make you revise it? This keeps the book’s call to responsibility connected to learning rather than a claim of having found the perfect diet.

A passage to read closely

Use money in a creative way like seeds; though it is totally artificial and man-made it can be used to the good when kept in healthy circulation.

Wendy E. Cook · Foodwise · Chapter 20 · PDF capture page 257.

Read the wording

A seed points beyond the immediate act to what may grow from it. Test the metaphor by naming a concrete effect and a limit; money cannot guarantee the outcome of every intention.

A worked example

A portfolio proposes a familiar rice, bean and vegetable meal, explains the source of each ingredient, and revises one unrealistic preparation step.

The revision demonstrates learning. A strong portfolio may disagree with a spiritual claim while accurately explaining it and making a thoughtful practical choice.

Three key takeaways

  • Connect all four parts of the book.
  • Use passages as reasons, not decoration.
  • Make your revision and remaining questions visible.
