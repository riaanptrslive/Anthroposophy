00 — Meet the person before the type

Prior course source: temperaments.mjs; prior reference: Capture pages 6–7; 19–20. . These references retain the old edition/marker system and are not newly verified PDF locators.

[INTERPRETATION — existing instructor teaching] Source paraphrases below remain distinguishable from a fresh [BOOK] extraction.

Learning objective

Use the four temperaments as a subject of study without reducing people to labels.

Core explanation

Steiner opens with the individuality of every person. A temperament is an underlying colouring of personality in his account, expressed through responses to the world. The four names describe basic patterns, but the lecture explicitly says that pure types do not occur in life. Its aim is practical understanding of encounters and development.

Context and qualifications

This short book contains one lecture, given in Berlin on 4 March 1909. Our lessons divide its argument into study sections; these are not chapters written by Steiner. We distinguish his spiritual explanation, the behaviour he describes and our own teaching examples. The source’s physiological and illness claims are historical claims, not a diagnostic system used by this course.

Example — instructor-created unless explicitly attributed

A usually quiet colleague becomes energetic when helping with a garden. Another, lively at lunch, works patiently on a repair. One label does not explain either person completely.

Explanation of the example

The example encourages attention to situations and mixtures. It does not establish that either colleague has one particular spiritual constitution.

Activity

Describe a fictional person in two different situations without using a temperament name. Separate what the person does from what you infer. Keep this description for the final lesson.

Questions and explained answers

Is the course organized around several original lectures?

No. It divides a single lecture into original teaching sections.

Does recognizing a pattern explain the whole person?

No. The lecture itself describes mixtures and individual differences.

What is a useful first notebook entry?

“She spoke little during introductions but asked detailed questions about the plants” describes behaviour. “She is incapable of interest” adds an unsupported judgment.

Teaching conclusion

A type may organize a question; the person remains the subject of understanding.

01 — Two streams and their meeting

Prior course source: temperaments.mjs; prior reference: Capture pages 7–12. . These references retain the old edition/marker system and are not newly verified PDF locators.

[INTERPRETATION — existing instructor teaching] Source paraphrases below remain distinguishable from a fresh [BOOK] extraction.

Learning objective

Explain why temperament is an intermediary in Steiner’s account.

Core explanation

The lecture brings together two streams: inherited characteristics from the ancestral line and the enduring individuality that Steiner says develops through repeated earthly lives. Temperament is their meeting, rather than a synonym for either heredity or individuality. Each stream gives a particular colouring to the other.

Context and qualifications

He develops the claim through comparisons involving minerals, plants, animals and human learning, and through the analogy of blue and yellow producing green. These comparisons make his proposal intelligible; they are not independent demonstrations of reincarnation. Differences between relatives alone cannot establish a previous life. The historical biological examples should not be adopted as a complete modern account of inheritance or embryology.

Example — instructor-created unless explicitly attributed

Two siblings share family habits but respond differently to learning an instrument. One eagerly tries many sounds; the other keeps returning to one difficult passage.

Explanation of the example

This makes the question of similarity and individuality concrete. The fictional contrast does not prove which traits are inherited, learned or carried from a former life.

Activity

Draw two arrows labelled “hereditary stream” and “individuality through repeated lives, in Steiner’s account,” meeting at “temperament.” Write what the diagram explains and what it does not prove.

Questions and explained answers

Why is temperament not simply heredity here?

It expresses a meeting of inherited constitution and the spiritual individuality proposed by Steiner.

What does the colour-mixing analogy contribute?

It pictures two contributions producing a distinctive result; it does not supply evidence that reincarnation occurs.

What should the diagram avoid?

Do not present its arrows as measured causal quantities or treat siblings’ differences as proof of a specific past life.

Teaching conclusion

Temperament mediates two streams within the lecture’s spiritual explanation.

02 — Four members, four tendencies

Prior course source: temperaments.mjs; prior reference: Capture pages 12–14. Also consult the corresponding passages in the parallel edition: the capture has gaps.. These references retain the old edition/marker system and are not newly verified PDF locators.

[INTERPRETATION — existing instructor teaching] Source paraphrases below remain distinguishable from a fresh [BOOK] extraction.

Learning objective

Learn the mapping without confusing predominance with exclusive possession.

Core explanation

Steiner describes the human being through physical body, etheric or life body, astral body and the bearer of the I. All four belong to the person in his account. A temperament arises when one predominates in their relationship: I-bearer with choleric, astral with sanguine, etheric with phlegmatic and physical with melancholic.

Context and qualifications

The physical member concerns bodily existence; the etheric concerns life and growth; the astral bears changing sensations, desires and representations; the I is associated with self-awareness. Predominance is not the absence of other members. The lecture also associates these members with blood circulation, nerves and glands. We study those correspondences as part of the text’s spiritual physiology, not as measurements that diagnose temperament.

Example — instructor-created unless explicitly attributed

A reader says, “The sanguine person has an astral body, so the others do not.” The classification has been turned into four different kinds of human being.

Explanation of the example

The correction is relational: all four members are present, but the source assigns a different emphasis within their combination.

Activity

Reconstruct the four-row map below from memory. Add a sentence explaining why neither a temperament name nor a bodily measurement establishes the whole spiritual account.

Questions and explained answers

Which pairing belongs to the phlegmatic type?

Predominance of the etheric or life body.

Does a choleric person have more human worth because the I predominates?

No. The mapping is not a hierarchy of human worth or an achievement scale.

How would you correct the fictional reader?

All four members belong to every person in the account; the sanguine pairing names predominance, not sole possession.

Teaching conclusion

The map concerns predominance within a shared fourfold constitution.

03 — Choleric: purpose meeting resistance

Prior course source: temperaments.mjs; prior reference: Capture pages 14; 16–17; 22–23. . These references retain the old edition/marker system and are not newly verified PDF locators.

[INTERPRETATION — existing instructor teaching] Source paraphrases below remain distinguishable from a fresh [BOOK] extraction.

Learning objective

Understand the movement from forceful insistence toward capable action.

Core explanation

The choleric picture centres on strong will and the wish to assert a purpose against obstacles. Steiner associates it with predominance of the I-bearer. One-sidedness appears when insistence narrows into a single aim that will not listen or adjust. Purposefulness and anger are not identical, even when the source discusses them together.

Context and qualifications

For the child, the lecture emphasizes respect for an educator’s competence and the encounter with real difficulty. Our teaching adaptation uses achievable challenges and demonstrable skill. It does not turn authority into infallibility or arrange humiliation and conflict. A teacher can model competence by noticing an error and correcting it honestly.

Example — instructor-created unless explicitly attributed

During a model-building activity, a learner insists the bridge must stand immediately. The educator calmly demonstrates a stable joint and offers a manageable construction problem.

Explanation of the example

Effort now meets a task that responds to skill rather than shouting. This illustrates redirection of energy, not a diagnosis of the learner.

Activity

Write two responses to the situation: one that enters a power struggle and one that offers a clear boundary plus a workable challenge. Explain the difference.

Questions and explained answers

What is central in the choleric picture?

A strong impulse to assert a purpose and overcome resistance.

Why is deliberate humiliation a poor adaptation?

It creates a conflict with the person rather than a useful encounter with a task, and is not needed to demonstrate competence.

What might a constructive response say?

“We will keep the materials on the table. Let us test this joint together, then you can try the next one.” It combines a boundary, help and a concrete challenge.

Teaching conclusion

Direct force toward a meaningful task that requires skill and adjustment.

04 — Sanguine: interest that moves quickly

Prior course source: temperaments.mjs; prior reference: Capture pages 14–15; 21–22. Also consult the corresponding passages in the parallel edition: the capture has gaps.. These references retain the old edition/marker system and are not newly verified PDF locators.

[INTERPRETATION — existing instructor teaching] Source paraphrases below remain distinguishable from a fresh [BOOK] extraction.

Learning objective

Explore how an existing interest can become a starting point for attention.

Core explanation

The sanguine picture is one of responsiveness to changing impressions. Interest is readily awakened but moves on. The lecture associates this with predominance of the astral body. The difficulty is not simply an absence of interest: it is the challenge of staying with something when another impression arrives.

Context and qualifications

Steiner’s educational proposal begins with affection for a trusted person and with things that already draw deeper interest. He also describes withdrawing and returning interesting objects. Our adaptation uses a predictable return to an activity, never withdrawal of affection or basic needs. The missing descriptive passage in the capture was checked against the parallel edition.

Example — instructor-created unless explicitly attributed

A learner starts three sketches, then turns to a new object. An educator notices a sustained interest in birds and invites one small drawing to be finished before the next choice.

Explanation of the example

The example starts from responsiveness already present. It adds a reachable ending rather than describing the learner as empty of attention.

Activity

Design a short activity with one existing interest, one small completion point and one planned return. State how you would check whether the arrangement actually helped this learner.

Questions and explained answers

Why is “no interest at all” inaccurate for this picture?

Interest arises quickly but may not remain attached to one impression.

What does the source emphasize in the relationship with the educator?

Affection and interest in a particular trusted person.

What would count as a useful observation after the activity?

Record whether one drawing reached the agreed stopping point and what support was needed. Do not treat success or difficulty as proof of a fixed type.

Teaching conclusion

Begin with a real interest and make the next act of attention concrete.

05 — Phlegmatic: finding an invitation to participate

Prior course source: temperaments.mjs; prior reference: Capture pages 15; 24–25. Also consult the corresponding passages in the parallel edition: the capture has gaps.. These references retain the old edition/marker system and are not newly verified PDF locators.

[INTERPRETATION — existing instructor teaching] Source paraphrases below remain distinguishable from a fresh [BOOK] extraction.

Learning objective

Follow the proposal that another person’s interest can awaken engagement.

Core explanation

Steiner associates the phlegmatic temperament with the etheric or life body and describes inward contentment that can leave external events at a distance. In the educational passage, the important route is companionship: the interests of other children become an invitation to participate. The contentment description is clearer in the parallel edition where the capture is blank.

Context and qualifications

The source distinguishes being directly drawn by an object from encountering that object through another person’s enthusiasm. Our example explores the second route. A quiet pace is not itself a defect, and participation can take many forms. Observe whether the person wishes to join rather than presume that every quiet observer needs correction.

Example — instructor-created unless explicitly attributed

A learner watches a seed-planting activity without joining. A companion explains a favourite plant and offers the learner a choice between filling a pot and making its label.

Explanation of the example

The invitation connects an activity with a relationship and a manageable contribution. It does not require competition, public pressure or assigning a permanent temperament.

Activity

Write a friendly invitation to a shared activity with two possible roles and room to decline. Describe what would show interest even if the learner remained quiet.

Questions and explained answers

What route does the source propose for the phlegmatic child?

Experiencing the varied interests of companions.

How can a quiet learner participate?

By choosing a role, asking a question, watching purposefully or completing a contribution without speaking much.

Why include room to decline?

The activity explores an invitation, not forced conformity. A refusal gives reason to ask what support or alternative would fit better.

Teaching conclusion

Shared interest can offer a route into participation without demanding a different personality.

06 — Melancholic: sensitivity and compassionate attention

Prior course source: temperaments.mjs; prior reference: Capture pages 15–16; 23–24. Also consult the corresponding passages in the parallel edition: the capture has gaps.. These references retain the old edition/marker system and are not newly verified PDF locators.

[INTERPRETATION — existing instructor teaching] Source paraphrases below remain distinguishable from a fresh [BOOK] extraction.

Learning objective

Understand the movement from personal burden toward concern for another.

Core explanation

In the melancholic picture, Steiner describes the physical body as offering resistance to the other members, experienced as burden and pain. His educational proposal draws on the credibility of someone who has known difficulty and on compassion for another person’s destiny. The intention is to work with sensitivity already present rather than dismiss it.

Context and qualifications

The lecture goes further in recommending encounters with suffering. Our exercise is a limited adaptation: acknowledge the learner’s experience and consider an ordinary, age-appropriate situation in which help is welcome. Do not manufacture distress or suggest that another person’s pain makes one’s own unimportant. “Melancholic” here names the lecture’s type; it is not a diagnosis of depression.

Example — instructor-created unless explicitly attributed

A learner is disappointed after missing a game. Instead of saying “others have it worse,” an educator listens and later invites help writing a kind message to a friend who also missed an event.

Explanation of the example

Concern for someone else can coexist with acknowledgment of one’s own disappointment. The example does not require forcing cheerfulness or seeking painful material.

Activity

Write a response with two parts: acknowledgment of a feeling and an optional, modest act of care. Explain how the second part avoids cancelling the first.

Questions and explained answers

What does the lecture ask the educator to start from?

The capacity for sensitivity and suffering already present, rather than treating it as something to beat out of a child.

Why is “others have it worse” inadequate?

It ranks suffering and can dismiss the learner’s experience instead of helping them understand another person.

What would a thoughtful response include?

“It mattered to you to be there. If you would like, we can send your friend a message together.” The invitation remains optional and does not deny the disappointment.

Teaching conclusion

Compassion can widen attention while leaving room for a person’s own experience.

07 — Mixtures, appearance and the limits of a label

Prior course source: temperaments.mjs; prior reference: Capture pages 16–21. Also consult the corresponding passages in the parallel edition: the capture has gaps.. These references retain the old edition/marker system and are not newly verified PDF locators.

[INTERPRETATION — existing instructor teaching] Source paraphrases below remain distinguishable from a fresh [BOOK] extraction.

Learning objective

Read the source’s descriptions without turning them into a sorting test.

Core explanation

The lecture says the four basic types do not appear in pure form and values the variety they bring to life. Its aim is to guide tendencies rather than make everyone alike. This should change how we use the preceding portraits: look for a pattern with exceptions, not a box that settles every question.

Context and qualifications

Steiner also makes claims about build, gait, facial expression and eye colour, and describes extreme outcomes with historical psychiatric language. These passages belong to the source and deserve explicit recognition. They do not provide a reliable basis here for assigning temperament from a face or body, predicting illness, or ranking intelligence. Our course uses fictional situations and revisable descriptions instead of a personality test.

Example — instructor-created unless explicitly attributed

Someone infers a forceful temperament from a photograph. A colleague supplies examples of the same person being hesitant, persistent, playful and patient in different situations.

Explanation of the example

The additional information shows why a visual label cannot replace attention to actions, context and the person’s own account.

Activity

Return to your fictional person. Add one example that challenges your first impression. Replace a fixed statement beginning “always” with a more precise description of when and how the behaviour occurs.

Questions and explained answers

Does the source propose eliminating all temperament differences?

No. It values diversity and seeks direction rather than uniformity.

Can an eye colour establish a person’s moral character?

No. This course does not use physical appearance to assign inner worth or fixed personality.

What makes a revision stronger?

“During rushed group decisions, he repeats his proposal; in practical work he listens carefully” gives conditions and a counterexample instead of “he never listens.”

Teaching conclusion

A useful description remains open to evidence that changes it.

08 — Education: work with what is already present

Prior course source: temperaments.mjs; prior reference: Capture pages 21–25. . These references retain the old edition/marker system and are not newly verified PDF locators.

[INTERPRETATION — existing instructor teaching] Source paraphrases below remain distinguishable from a fresh [BOOK] extraction.

Learning objective

Compare the four routes proposed in the lecture.

Core explanation

The educational principle is to begin with an existing capacity instead of trying to force a missing quality into someone. The source gives four routes: affection and personal interest for the sanguine child; respect for competence and meaningful resistance for the choleric; sympathy with another’s destiny for the melancholic; and companions’ interests for the phlegmatic.

Context and qualifications

These routes make the book’s general principle concrete, but they are proposals within this lecture, not guaranteed outcomes for classified children. All children can need affection, capable guidance, companionship and understanding. Our adaptation treats each route as a possibility to consider in a particular situation, with attention to what the learner actually says and does.

Example — instructor-created unless explicitly attributed

Four learners hesitate over the same group project for different reasons. One needs a familiar interest, another a worthwhile challenge, another acknowledgment of disappointment, and another an inviting role alongside a friend.

Explanation of the example

Different support can serve a shared learning aim. The response should follow the situation, not a permanent assignment of each child to a type.

Activity

Choose a fictional project and two possible supports. For each, name the existing capacity, the invitation and the observable response that would lead you to continue or revise it.

Questions and explained answers

What do the four routes have in common?

Each tries to develop through something already present rather than through reproach or force.

Are affection and companionship reserved for particular types?

No. The lecture emphasizes them differently, but that is not a reason to withhold them from others.

What should prompt revision of your plan?

The learner’s response: confusion, refusal, strain or a different interest may show that the proposed support does not fit.

Teaching conclusion

Begin with a capacity you can identify, then check whether your response fits the person.

09 — Self-education through an indirect route

Prior course source: temperaments.mjs; prior reference: Capture pages 25–27. . These references retain the old edition/marker system and are not newly verified PDF locators.

[INTERPRETATION — existing instructor teaching] Source paraphrases below remain distinguishable from a fresh [BOOK] extraction.

Learning objective

Distinguish useful arrangement from repeated self-reproach.

Core explanation

In the final practical passage, Steiner argues that simply ordering oneself to have a different temperament accomplishes little. Reason can instead arrange suitable situations: short-lived interest where it has a place, a task where rage is ineffective, concern directed toward another, or, in his phlegmatic example, boredom brought to an extreme.

Context and qualifications

We preserve the distinction between direct self-reproach and indirect arrangement without prescribing every historical suggestion. Our activity uses a small chosen task and a review point. It does not deliberately provoke anger, seek distress or impose prolonged boredom. The question is how to give a tendency a workable expression while retaining responsibility for action.

Example — instructor-created unless explicitly attributed

A learner repeatedly says “I must stop changing subjects.” Instead, they choose a five-minute comparison task in which looking at three examples is useful, followed by one written conclusion.

Explanation of the example

The arrangement gives variety a place and still asks for completion. Whether it helps must be assessed from the result, not assumed from a temperament label.

Activity

Choose one ordinary habit of your own, or use the fictional example. Design a small task with a purpose, a limit and a review question. State what you will change if it does not help.

Questions and explained answers

Does the lecture say reason has no role?

No. It gives reason an indirect role in choosing situations and experiences.

Does a tendency excuse harmful behaviour?

No. Understanding a tendency does not remove responsibility for how one acts toward others.

What makes the small task reviewable?

It has an identifiable action and stopping point, such as comparing three examples and writing one conclusion, so the learner can assess what happened.

Teaching conclusion

Use thought to arrange a useful practice, then learn from the result.

10 — Your synthesis: a person, a pattern, a response

Prior course source: temperaments.mjs; prior reference: Capture pages 6–27. Also consult the corresponding passages in the parallel edition: the capture has gaps.. These references retain the old edition/marker system and are not newly verified PDF locators.

[INTERPRETATION — existing instructor teaching] Source paraphrases below remain distinguishable from a fresh [BOOK] extraction.

Learning objective

Bring source understanding and individual attention together.

Core explanation

The lecture moves from the riddle of the individual through a spiritual account of temperament to education and self-education. The closing passage, missing from the capture and checked in the parallel edition, connects understanding with responsive human love. Its fourfold map helps organize the reading, while its recognition of mixtures prevents the portraits from becoming complete descriptions of people. The practical principle is to work with an existing capacity.

Context and qualifications

Your final case should distinguish what is observed, what the source proposes and what you choose to do. Explain one temperament pattern accurately, include a counterexample and design a response that can be revised. Agreement with reincarnation or a claim to diagnose a person is not required to demonstrate understanding.

Example — instructor-created unless explicitly attributed

A learner revises “my fictional teammate is always forceful” into a description of insistence during rushed decisions and patience during careful construction. They propose a specific role and ask for the teammate’s view.

Explanation of the example

The pattern informs a question, while the actual person and situation determine the response. The revised account is more useful because it can be corrected.

Activity

Use your opening fictional case. Write 150–250 words or give an equivalent spoken account: describe two situations, explain one source concept with its reading reference, state a limit of the classification and propose one respectful, reviewable response.

Questions and explained answers

Which parts of the lecture should your synthesis connect?

The individual, the proposed basis of temperament, mixtures and the practical principle of working with what is present.

What prevents the case from becoming a diagnosis?

It uses specific observations, a fictional example, explicit limits and a response open to correction rather than claiming a fixed inner constitution.

What should you revise if the person’s response differs from your prediction?

Reconsider the description and proposed support. Do not dismiss the response merely to protect the temperament label.

Teaching conclusion

Understanding a type is worthwhile when it improves attention to a particular person.
