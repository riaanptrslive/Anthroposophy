Theosophy

Source: C:/Users/riaan/Downloads/Teosophy/Theosophy -- Rudolf Steiner -- 1971 -- Anthroposophic Press -- 35a0e3779e08323bf09813009f1f2dca -- Anna’s Archive.pdf.

228 PDF pages. Title and copyright pages identify Anthroposophic Press, 1971; Henry B. Monges, revised by Gilbert Church. All pages were surveyed as contact sheets for illustrations, not reread sequentially. No standalone illustrated threefold/fourfold human-being plate was found in this scan. The relevant visual arrangement is a printed list.

PDF pages 64–66 were rendered and read at larger size. Printed page 36 = PDF page 64; printed page 37 = PDF page 65; printed page 38 = PDF page 66. The nine-member list on p. 36 is legible in the scan and verifies the list markers damaged in the earlier transcription. C and D are grouped together, as are F and G, for a seven-member account; its list continues on p. 37. The simplified fourfold account appears later on p. 37, continuing into p. 38. These are related groupings, not interchangeable lists.

Site asset: docs/assets/book-diagrams/theosophy-nine-members.png. Cropped from the PDF p. 64 render at 1500 pixels high, rectangle (355,535)–(710,885). Only the nine-member list is included. No retouching, recolouring or reconstruction. Added after the comparison explanation in the bilingual human-constitution reference; the caption points readers to the fuller discussion in Theosophy Lessons 3–6. An accessible text equivalent names every member.

Colour

Source: C:/Users/riaan/Downloads/Colour/Colour.pdf.

158 PDF pages; an image-based, multi-column capture, not a facsimile preserving the book's printed pagination. The end contains capture-software material. Frontmatter on PDF p. 2 identifies Rudolf Steiner Press, translation copyright 1992, ISBN 9781855842755; John Salter translated Lectures 1–3 and Pauline Wehrle translated Lectures 4–12. It matches the earlier 158-marker source capture in structure. Page numbers used below are PDF/capture numbers only.

All 158 pages were surveyed as contact sheets for images. Diagram candidates occur on PDF pages 22, 26, 29, 61, 72, 75, 82, 83, 85, 90, 98, 108, 109, 123–125, 131, 137, 139–141 and 143. Candidate locations are an inventory, not a claim that each image has been cleared for teaching or its context fully reviewed. Pages 2, 22, 26, 29, 146 and 147 were inspected at larger size for this update. Teaching diagrams in this capture are monochrome; no watercolor plate suitable for the human-being introduction was found. The frontmatter credits cover art to Steiner, but the artwork itself is not available as a suitable plate in this capture.

Two selected diagrams are now in the bilingual Colour course:

  • Lesson 1: the image-colour cycle, lecture of 6 May 1921, GA 291, PDF p. 22. Asset colour-image-cycle.png, rectangle (100,395)–(515,795) from a page render scaled to 1800 pixels wide. Full labels and arrows retained. Caption explains the four image-colour relationships and distinguishes them from the four human members.
  • Lesson 2: the yellow/red/blue painting gestures, lecture of 7 May 1921, GA 291, PDF p. 26. Asset colour-yellow-red-blue.png, rectangle (1225,52)–(1755,235) from a page render scaled to 1800 pixels wide. All three forms and labels retained. Caption explains that black hatching represents intensity of the named colour, not an instruction to add black pigment.

The diagrams are described as reproductions in the supplied edition. This review does not establish that their printed lines are surviving original blackboard marks by Steiner. Images are preserved in monochrome and displayed at no more than their extracted dimensions, with full-image links and bilingual explanations. Existing digital teaching studies remain visibly separate.
