    3. Concept Dictionary

    The following definitions, where present, are retained instructor formulations. They are [INTERPRETATION — prior teaching], to be collated with the source's exact context before marking them [BOOK].

    • GA 10 — the bibliographic identifier for this book.
    • Supersensible — beyond ordinary sensory perception, in Steiner’s terminology.
    • Reverence — serious respect for what one seeks to know.
    • Judgment — evaluating a claim and its grounds.
    • Inner tranquillity — a deliberate interval of collected attention.
    • Self-observation — examining one’s activity rather than only its external result.
    • Probation — the preparatory stage in this account.
    • Assent — accepting a statement as true.
    • Enlightenment — a stage of developing spiritual perception in the book.
    • Potential — a capacity that has not yet unfolded into its later form.
    • Dignity — the worth of a person beyond their use to another.
    • Patience — sustained effort without demanding immediate results.
    • Initiation — entry into a form of higher knowledge in the book.
    • Presence of mind — collected responsiveness in a situation requiring action.
    • Equanimity — steadiness through changing feelings and circumstances.
    • Tact — consideration in how and when one speaks.
    • Condition — a prerequisite proposed by the author for training.
    • Participation — belonging to and affecting a shared situation.
    • Resolution — a considered intention to act.
    • Gratitude — recognition of what one’s life receives from beyond oneself.
    • Lotus flower — a comparative name for a purported spiritual organ.
    • Impartiality — readiness to encounter something beyond one’s existing opinion.
    • Etheric body — the supersensible life organization in this account.
    • Higher self — the spiritual individuality described as entering conscious activity.
    • Dream image — pictorial content of a dream.
    • Authority — a claim to provide a dependable account for others.
    • Continuity of consciousness — the proposed extension of awareness through sleep.
    • Analogy — a comparison that explains one aspect without making two things identical.
    • Thinking — forming and relating ideas.
    • Willing — the orientation toward and activity of action.
    • Threshold — the boundary of entry into the described supersensible experience.
    • Karma — the connection of deeds and destiny in Steiner’s spiritual account.
    • Service — directing one’s capacities toward the good of others.
    • Hierarchy — an ordering into higher and lower ranks.
    • Pure thinking — thinking considered apart from sensory content in the author’s argument.
    • Self-consciousness — awareness of oneself within an experience.
    • Synthesis — a connected account that preserves relevant distinctions.
    • Source support — passages that actually sustain the explanation being offered.
