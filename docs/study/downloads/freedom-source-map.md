# The Philosophy of Freedom — new source map

**Working witness:** the user's 147-page, three-column capture named *The philosophy of freedomThe basis of freedom.pdf*. Earlier research identifies the Wilson translation, but this capture lacks its title/imprint, foreword, translation introduction and the start of the 1918 preface. That boundary was checked visually. Do not assign this file's PDF numbers to a printed edition. All fourteen chapters, their additions, the concluding section, appendix and endnotes have now been read sequentially from newly generated OCR. All-page contact review found no obvious coloured text highlights; fine or faint marks are not certified. Page 127’s column order was checked against its image.

**Second witness:** *The Essential Philosophy of Freedom*, Third Expanded Edition, edited and newly translated by Frederick Amrine, copyright 2022; 70 PDF capture pages. Its title, imprint, contents and Amrine preface have been checked. The preface expressly describes an abridgement that removes discussions Amrine considers dated and foregrounds striking formulations. Six appendices include editorial background and selections from Olin D. Wannamaker's 1963 commentary. This is not an interchangeable transcription of the fuller book. Its full supplied text has now been read and mapped in the [edition comparison](freedom-essential-comparison.md), including all six appendices. This is a substantive comparison, not a word-by-word collation.

## Source voices and gaps

Steiner's arguments, the 1918 additions, translator's explanations, Amrine's selection/commentary and Wannamaker's later exposition require separate attribution. A quotation of Spinoza, Kant or Hartmann here records how this book presents that thinker; independent primary-source comparison is a further research task.

**Needed upload:** the missing opening pages of the fuller edition, preferably as the complete PDF. The chapters already present can be processed independently. The Essential edition cannot silently supply the missing foreword or translation notes.

**OCR issues:** the capture has three columns. On PDF 127 its final words were pulled into the middle of a paragraph by OCR; the image has now established the correct column continuation. Several running headers and initial letters are damaged. The page 108 image confirms that the cross-reference itself says Chapter 13 while discussing purpose (the topic of Chapter 11). This is an apparent source cross-reference error, not an OCR correction; it is retained explicitly. A title in an endnote is not evidence that its cited book has been read.
