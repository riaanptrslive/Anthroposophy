Library close-reading method

Adopted 17 September 2026 from the user's supplied role and Francis Edmunds example. This is the working method for the whole course library, not a claim that all books have already received this treatment.

Source and teaching structure

READ → EXTRACT → EXPLAIN → EXPAND → CONNECT → TEACH.

Keep two connected structures: a source-order book map and a question-led lesson architecture. A chapter can need several lessons. A thematic lesson can draw on several chapters, but its source crosswalk must retain their distinct arguments. The supplied Edmunds example has detailed Chapter 1 notes and a proposed later course; it does not certify close reading of its later chapters.

Provenance

  • [BOOK]: checked in the actual identified source; name the speaker and give a page, section or paragraph locator.
  • [INTERPRETATION]: an inference or explanatory reconstruction; state the textual basis and the step added by the teacher.
  • [EXPANSION]: external context or an original teaching example. Cite researched external claims. Clearly distinguish proposed research from completed research.
  • [TEXT UNCERTAIN]: unreadable, interrupted or conflicting text. Do not silently repair it.
  • Not established in the supplied text. Use this where a causal mechanism, historical assertion, intention or conclusion lacks support.

Existing course explanations are prior teaching material. Migration into a knowledge base does not turn them into newly checked source evidence. Prior editorial records have dated claims and old pagination; these remain traceable but must be reconciled with current files.

Source gate

Verify the internal title, authorship, translation, edition and actual contents. A filename is not proof of identity. Count PDF capture pages separately from printed pages, Kindle locations and old Markdown markers. An array of empty page records is not an OCR transcription. Inspect illustrations and flattened highlights visually; zero PDF annotation objects does not mean zero highlights. Preserve full books and working OCR outside public content.

Book map before drafting

Record front matter, every chapter and substantive subsection, appendices, notes and bibliography. For each, identify its purpose, questions, concepts, arguments, examples, people, events, qualifications and relation to surrounding sections. Assign stable concept and highlight IDs. Do not mistake an advertisement, capture screen or table-of-contents entry for substantive source text.

Chapter record: fifteen required components

  1. Chapter purpose.
  2. Detailed sequential section notes.
  3. Major ideas, with author claim, explanation and significance.
  4. Arguments: premises, reasons, evidence, assumptions, inference, conclusion and qualifications.
  5. Examples and evidence: what happens, relevant details, explanatory role and limits.
  6. People, places, events and institutions; distinguish reported history from independently verified history.
  7. Important descriptive details.
  8. Mechanisms: starting condition → process → intermediate stages → outcome → consequences. If only a correspondence is supplied, do not invent a causal mechanism.
  9. Distinctions and comparisons.
  10. Interesting details worth teaching, selected for explanatory value.
  11. Background expansion needed, with research status.
  12. Idea dossiers: core idea, source account, background, origins, development, people, mechanism, example, limitation, debate, misconception, connections and teaching use. Record absent information explicitly rather than filling every field with speculation.
  13. Connections to earlier material; similarity is not documented influence.
  14. Potential course material: question, objectives, concepts, sources, explanation, example, people, misconception, complication, connections, discussion, exercise and research.
  15. Questions and uncertainties.

Cumulative book record

Maintain Book Map; Chapter Notes; Concept Dictionary; People Index; Timeline/Event Index; Argument Index; Example/Case Study Bank; Interesting Detail Bank; Idea Dossiers; Cross-Chapter Connections; Potential Lesson Bank; Questions Requiring Research. Add synthesis only when the source reading supports it. An outline is not a completed chapter.

Highlights and coverage

Split each highlight into its distinct ideas and include unhighlighted context. Map each idea to an actual explanation, not a passing mention. Track unhighlighted substantive concepts too. Report explained, partly explained, deferred and unavailable material separately. A working link or sufficient word count does not prove explanatory coverage.

Teaching and quality control

Use Question → Context → Problem → Explanation → Evidence → Example → Complication → Significance → Connection. Reconstruct spiritual claims fairly in their own context; do not reduce them silently to everyday psychology or present them as established science. Distinguish author, editor, translator, commentator and instructor. Recheck coverage, fidelity, provenance, depth, teaching value, connections and unsupported assertions before releasing a new lesson. Preserve the existing English and Portuguese courses while source work is in progress; a new English research record is not a completed bilingual course revision.
