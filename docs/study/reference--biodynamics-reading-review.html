Biodynamics course: source and teaching review

Reviewed 15 September 2026. Source: the user-supplied What Is Biodynamics? A Way to Heal and Revitalize the Earth. Seven Lectures, Rudolf Steiner, SteinerBooks. Hugh J. Courtney’s introduction carries a 2005 copyright. Marcia Merryman Means edited the collection and wrote the brief introductions to each lecture (capture 3). The main introduction heading on capture 5 is “So That the Earth May Be One: An Introduction to Biodynamic Agriculture”; the contents wording differs. The course uses “Courtney’s introduction” to avoid conflating them.

Teaching decisions

  • Nine lessons preserve the seven-section book structure, adding substantial introduction study and a final portfolio. The long introduction and agriculture discussions may be read over multiple sessions.
  • Courtney’s reports, ninefold Christian-esoteric interpretation and disaster claims remain his own contributions. They are not silently presented as Steiner’s words or as demonstrated causal findings.
  • The lessons explain etheric life, cycles, planetary spirit, elemental cooperation and complementary forms before agricultural applications. Correspondence diagrams are explicitly attributed models.
  • The compost lesson covers all six preparations (502–507), their materials, sheaths and claimed roles. Orientation maps 500–508; Agriculture I explains the two horn preparations; Agriculture III includes horsetail and the historical ash proposals.
  • The course retains the tension between invitations to experiment and Steiner’s claim of inward certainty (especially captures 114 and 118–119). It does not selectively recast him as speaking only provisionally.
  • Discussion qualifications remain visible: ordinary manuring continues; practical quantities and timings require investigation; the oak calcium figure refers to ash analysis according to the note; the exact mesentery portion is uncertain.
  • The book disputes botanical fertilization and minimizes microbial causation. OpenStax and USDA NRCS are linked beside these specific comparisons. University of Minnesota Extension provides plant-disease context. Sources checked on the review date.
  • Assignments involve source reading, ordinary observation and proposals on paper. They do not require preparing animal materials, grinding silica, killing specimens, tasting plants, changing diet or disrupting sleep.
  • Rubrics assess accurate understanding, source use and reasoned judgment. Agreement with spiritual claims is not required for full marks.
