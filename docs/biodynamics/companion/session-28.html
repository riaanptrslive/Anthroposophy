Source commentary · PDF capture 108

Male red deer and perennial nettle clarified

Inspect this page’s inventory

The questions clarify a male red deer and Urtica dioica. Rain management is a balance: complete exclusion and excessive leaching are both criticized, with peat offered as a possible moderation. Crucially, the preparations are said to encourage weeds as well as crops because they promote plant growth generally. Weed suppression therefore needs a different account, deferred to Agriculture III. The Krantz manure method prompts acknowledgment of possible benefits and a reservation about short-term success becoming less effective over time. This is an uncertainty, not a demonstrated failure of the method. It continues the chapter's stated intention to supplement ordinary practice rather than replace every existing technique.

Source commentary · PDF capture 109

Heating versus loose storage

Inspect this page’s inventory

Steiner separates useful heating from his uncertainty about loose piling and complete odor removal. A high pile on ordinary ground is distinguished from a pile on an elevated site because the relation to underlying forces matters in his model. Specific vineyard and orchard adaptations are promised later in the original course and are not fully supplied by this anthology. He rejects paving as a separation from soil and distinguishes sandy, clayey and loess conditions, proposing balance of permeability and solidity. These are historical recommendations explained by the lecture's soil-contact model, not a contemporary manure-storage specification. The unanswered crop-specific details must remain marked as outside the supplied selection.

Source commentary · PDF capture 110

Conflicting dandelion/yarrow fodder reports

Inspect this page’s inventory

Participants give conflicting accounts of dandelion and yarrow as fodder. Steiner calls for observation but also confidently asserts that animals avoid harmful plants. That universal reassurance is not sound feeding guidance; E8 documents livestock poisoning and the need for identification. His yeast analogy makes a more limited point: a substance's effect depends on dose, use and context, so an ingredient need not be eaten alone to contribute beneficially to a process. The discussion leaves regional and developmental differences insufficiently resolved. Only a small area of the herbs is said to be needed because the preparations use tiny quantities. Preserve both the demand to investigate and the overconfident instinct claim.

Scientific comparison · Livestock and poisonous plants

Livestock can be poisoned by plants, including material in forage. Instinct is not a dependable guarantee of safety. This directly limits the lecture’s universal reassurance that animals avoid what harms them.
