Source commentary · PDF capture 111

Storage after mixing

Inspect this page’s inventory

The preparation is to be mixed into manure before field application; after mixing, the duration is not presented as critical. Burial sites for the separate preparations should be spaced apart, while placement in finished manure may be near one another. Steiner explains this difference through the direction and interference of radiations. Material should be surrounded rather than exposed at the surface; distribution through several holes is favored. Asked about combining everything in one hole, he refuses certainty despite a possible theoretical argument. This is a clear example of a practical qualification within an otherwise confident cosmology. Quercus robur is then specified for the oak preparation.

Source commentary · PDF capture 112

Living outer bark

Inspect this page’s inventory

Living outer bark, fertile topsoil and exposure to frost are preferred; frost is interpreted as a time of stronger cosmic influence. The quartz question addresses fineness of material, not proof of efficacy. The nutrition challenge returns to the earlier claim that food enables assimilation from air: Steiner compares food's role with stretching a tight glove before inserting a hand. The analogy clarifies his distinction between enabling force and supplied substance but does not demonstrate it physiologically. E7 gives the contrasting digestive account. Notes identify Kolisko and the chamomile names. Understanding historical preparation details here does not require handling animal organs or grinding mineral powders.

Scientific comparison · Digestion and bodily substance

Digestion breaks food into nutrients that are absorbed and used for energy, growth and repair. This differs from the account that largely assigns bodily substance to refined sensory/atmospheric uptake. Historical protein quantities and therapeutic remarks in the book are not dietary or medical guidance.

Source commentary · PDF capture 113

77 percent refers to ash analysis

Inspect this page’s inventory

The editorial notes materially change how earlier passages should be taught. The 77-percent figure concerns oak ash, and Quercus robur is distinguished from American red oak. The mesentery is explained as a fold associated with the peritoneum, but the editor explicitly says the exact portion Steiner intended is unclear. That uncertainty must survive any modern diagram or recipe. The notes assign 502–507 to the compost preparations, discuss deer nomenclature and explain loess. These are clarifications by the editorial apparatus, not additional observations reported by Steiner during the lecture. A careful course uses the note to qualify the main text instead of repeating its compressed wording uncritically.

Teaching example — introduced by the instructor

A student reads 77 percent as fresh-bark composition. The ash-analysis note changes what was measured and prevents a large factual error.
