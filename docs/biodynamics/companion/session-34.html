Source commentary · PDF capture 128

Stag-bladder substitute unknown

Inspect this page’s inventory

Steiner does not know a suitable stag-bladder substitute among European animals and discourages immediately replacing it, while allowing investigation elsewhere. Insect timing also requires tests. His phrase about Venus being “behind” the Sun creates an ambiguity resolved tentatively by the editor on page 130. Other answers distinguish a negligible potato-top effect, peritoneum from tripe, and broad scattering from exact point placement. Fruit-crop details are promised for the following original lecture, outside this selection. Ordinary manuring should continue in unspecified matters. The page contains a combination of firm distinctions, possible extensions and acknowledged gaps; a study companion should preserve each category.

Source commentary · PDF capture 129

Continue proven methods

Inspect this page’s inventory

Steiner says to retain practices that have stood the test of time, while predicting that the new approach will eventually eliminate mineral fertilizers and reduce manure frequency. The distinction is immediate continuity versus a projected future transition, not an immediate command to stop fertilizing. Sheep and pig manure are said to modify the effect. The queen-breeding example illustrates his demand for long time horizons; it is a warning, not verification of the preparations. Larval and adult stages receive different proposed celestial timings. The notes clarify that the visible Scorpion constellation is intended despite the word “sign,” connecting back to Courtney's calendar distinction without proving agricultural efficacy.

Source commentary · PDF capture 130

Hollow chamomile head identification

Inspect this page’s inventory

The editor offers a hollow flower-head center as a stronger chamomile distinction and leaves the identity of the rejected garden chamomile uncertain. “Behind the Sun” is interpreted temporally—setting later as an evening star—because spatial alignment behind the Sun would defeat visible observation. The wording is an editorial reconstruction, marked as likely, rather than an unambiguous instruction in Steiner's answer. The eight-year recurrence of Venus positions explains the proposed timing context, but does not establish an effect on mice. The Bees reference points outward to another lecture series. This page demonstrates why notes belong in a substantive source map: they prevent practical and interpretive misunderstandings in the lecture text.
