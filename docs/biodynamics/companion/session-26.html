Source commentary · PDF capture 100

Substitution possible except nettle

Inspect this page’s inventory

Steiner allows substitutes in principle, with nettle later identified as the exception. Yarrow exemplifies sulfur organizing the relation of major organic elements to potash and plant structure. The image of spirit moistening its fingers with sulfur makes sulfur an organizer in his spiritual chemistry. Nature spirits are explicitly invoked here, an important anchor for Courtney's broader elemental interpretation. Yarrow's wild presence is praised, and its effect on astral weakness is asserted, but no clinical evidence is provided. The preparation uses the flower clusters. Read the material choice as following the proposed sulfur/potassium process; the later sheath choice requires a second argument, supplied on page 101.

Source commentary · PDF capture 101

Yarrow summer/winter preparation

Inspect this page’s inventory

Summer exposure and winter burial are intended to bring the yarrow material into changing environmental relationships before a small amount is added to manure. Steiner claims a wide radiating effect, invoking radium by analogy; analogy with a known radiation does not establish that this preparation radiates comparably. The intended outcome is renewed soil receptivity to subtle inputs.

Your highlighted passage supplies five connected ideas. The deer is oriented toward Earth's cosmic surroundings; antlers express its outward relation, as explained in Agriculture I; a kidney-to-bladder process is said to preserve what yarrow carries; the bladder's material constitution is essential to that process; and a thin membrane can embody extensive forces, pictured as a replica of the cosmos. Together these claims explain why a stag bladder is selected rather than a neutral bag. The continuation on page 102 contrasts the inwardly organized cow and says the sheath enhances yarrow's sulfur relationships. No measured mechanism establishing the claimed cosmic replication is given.

Source commentary · PDF capture 102

Cow/deer contrast and bladder enhancement

Inspect this page’s inventory

The deer/cow contrast completes the bladder argument: different animal organizations supposedly make different organs appropriate. “Within the living” means that the treatment follows organic relationships, even when the materials are no longer parts of living animals. Your highlight then makes a precise comparison: yarrow chiefly addresses potash, while calcium requires another plant. This is not a generic statement that both herbs are beneficial.

Chamomile supplies the added calcium relationship while still involving potash and sulfur. Steiner connects this with restraining harmful fructification and preserving health. He chooses cattle intestine because of the claimed process chamomile undergoes in an animal or person, contrasting it with the bladder's role for yarrow. Winter burial in humus-rich earth supplies the corresponding environmental phase. The causal links are presented as spiritual research; the page does not demonstrate that a sheath produces a quantified change in plant-available calcium.

Source commentary · PDF capture 103

Chamomile snow/sun setting, nitrogen retention and plant health

Inspect this page’s inventory

Snow and sunlight over the buried chamomile are meant to combine earthly winter conditions with cosmic astral influences. Steiner predicts nitrogen retention, soil enlivening and healthier plants. The mountain-railway story answers ridicule by showing that an initially mocked idea can succeed; it cannot establish that this particular proposal succeeds. He allows dried yarrow or chamomile and possible substitutes, though preferring the named plants. Nettle is different: it is claimed to be irreplaceable because it joins sulfur, potash, calcium and an iron radiation, compared with the human heart. The local iron example begins a practical qualification developed next: changing iron's influence is not necessarily removing all iron from soil.
