How you will learn

Read an explanation, examine the original passage, work through an example and apply the ideas. Three questions with explained answers help you revise your understanding. Allow 25–40 minutes per lesson plus reading time; divide the long introduction and agricultural discussions into several sessions.

This is a reading course for adults. It teaches the book’s spiritual framework and practical proposals with clear attribution. Activities use observation, diagrams and paper study proposals. No preparation making or special spiritual experience is required.

Use your copy for the full chapters. The final portfolio brings every lecture together in a portrait of a garden or farm. Notes can be saved in this browser and exported; assignments are not uploaded by the site.

Edition, voices and page references

Rudolf Steiner, What Is Biodynamics? A Way to Heal and Revitalize the Earth. Seven Lectures. SteinerBooks. Introduction © 2005 Hugh J. Courtney; edited by Marcia Merryman Means, who also wrote the brief introductions before the lectures.

Spiritual Beings I–II are lectures 1–2 of GA 136 (1912). Elementals I–II are lectures 7–8 of GA 230 (1923), revised translation by Matthew Barton. Agriculture I–III are lectures 4–6 of GA 327 (1924), translated by Catherine Creeger and Malcolm Gardner, with notes by Gardner. This is a selection from three series, not the complete Agriculture Course.

Page references count the 135 captures of the supplied PDF, beginning with the cover. They are not printed pages or Kindle locations. The accompanying OCR contains gaps around some highlighted text; selected quotations were checked against the page images. Consult the complete book where the captured text is interrupted. Line-break hyphens are removed.

Each quotation names its speaker. Portuguese passages are new study translations made for this course and appear beside the English original. The sources below offer specific biological comparisons, not confirmation of spiritual beings or preparation efficacy; checked 15 September 2026.

Bring this study into everyday life

Study a garden through relationships, observation and the book’s spiritual account.

At home

Observe one place over time: soil, plants, weather and care. Distinguish your record from the explanation proposed by the author.

As an educator

Use a garden or container-growing project to practise observation and responsibility. Explain what a small comparison can and cannot establish.

These are original course applications. Learn the source concepts, use the lesson’s worked example, then revisit a practice across separate occasions. Keep the author’s account and what you observed distinct.
