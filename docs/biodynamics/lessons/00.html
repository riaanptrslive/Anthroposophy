Understand the chapter

The key ideas, explained

Courtney introduces biodynamics through three connected ideas: the farm as an organism or individuality, its relationship with cosmic rhythms, and the use of preparations. “Individuality” asks us to consider a particular place: its soil, plants, animals, seasonal conditions and human work. A farm becomes intelligible through these relationships. The preparation system is especially important to Courtney; he argues that accounts which stop at the farm-organism idea leave out the distinctive practice.

The introduction surveys preparations 500–508: two horn preparations, six compost preparations and horsetail. Courtney advocates using the complete set, while acknowledging disagreement about the classification of horsetail. He also recounts results from his own work and discusses planting calendars. These are different kinds of material: a description of a practice, a personal report of its effects, and an explanation of why it might work. Learning the system requires keeping all three visible.

Courtney’s later pages interpret agriculture through Christian esotericism, ninefold correspondences, subnature and the redemption of elemental beings. His claims about preparations and disasters belong to that interpretation; they are not demonstrated by the anecdotes. This anthology then presents seven Steiner lectures selected from three larger series. Marcia Merryman Means supplies the short introductions before each lecture. Courtney’s 2005 interpretation, Means’s summaries and Steiner’s 1912–1924 words must retain their own authorship.

Connect the parts without confusing their authors

The anthology’s route has a logic: learn a way of attending to nature, encounter a spiritual account of nature’s activities, and then consider agricultural interventions based on that account. Courtney adds his own interpretation of preparations and their religious significance. Means introduces the selected lectures. These contributions help frame the reading, but they do not turn every sentence in the volume into a statement by Steiner.

Use a particular garden as a continuing example. First describe what is present: soil, water, plants, surrounding habitats and human work. Next identify which relationship the book asks you to notice. Finally state the spiritual interpretation or intervention being proposed and who proposes it. A description of a preparation tells you what the practice is; an account of a successful harvest reports an outcome; a theory explains why the outcome is attributed to the practice. Keeping these three steps distinct is essential to both the opening reading and the final project.

A passage to read closely

It is only with persistent questioning that the use of certain herbal preparations might be revealed.

Hugh J. Courtney · What Is Biodynamics? · Introduction · PDF capture page 5.

Read the wording

“Persistent questioning” describes Courtney’s concern about how biodynamics is explained to newcomers. He wants readers to ask what practitioners actually do. The sentence introduces his emphasis; it does not establish that the preparations produce the claimed effects.

A worked example

A learner draws a garden with compost, vegetable beds, water, insects and the gardener. She adds arrows for plant residues returning to compost and harvested food leaving the garden.

This map begins the organism perspective. To represent Courtney’s account fully, she adds a separate layer for seasonal and cosmic rhythms and marks where preparations enter. Labeling that layer as the book’s interpretation avoids confusing a relationship diagram with evidence of every proposed force.

Three key takeaways

  • Study relationships and the particular character of a place.
  • Preparations are central to Courtney’s definition.
  • Attribute introduction, editorial summaries and lectures separately.

Check your understanding

Why is an organism diagram insufficient for Courtney?

Show an explained answer

It can show relationships while omitting preparations and cosmic rhythms, both central to his account.

Who wrote the paragraphs before each lecture?

Show an explained answer

Marcia Merryman Means wrote the short editorial introductions. They should not be quoted as Steiner’s lecture text.

Does an account of rain after spraying prove the spray caused it?

Show an explained answer

No. The sequence is a report. Establishing causation requires comparison and attention to alternative explanations.
