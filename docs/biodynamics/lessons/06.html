Agriculture II: the six compost preparations

Explain the six-part preparation system and why its materials, sheaths and processes matter within the book.

Understand the chapter

The key ideas, explained

This lecture develops the idea that taking produce away from a farm draws upon processes that must be renewed. Ordinary manuring is retained, but Steiner proposes small additions that would help manure retain and organize the substances plants require. He presents the preparations as catalysts of relationships, not as large additions of fertilizer nutrients. His word “living” includes etheric and cosmic processes, as well as recognizable organic materials.

The sequence is yarrow (502), chamomile (503), nettle (504), oak bark (505), dandelion (506) and valerian (507). In Steiner’s explanation, yarrow relates sulfur and potassium; chamomile helps stabilize nitrogen and involves calcium processes; nettle organizes the manure’s sensitivity; oak bark brings a calcium-related balancing process; dandelion mediates silica and potassium; valerian relates to phosphorus. These are the roles claimed in the text. The table below gives the corresponding materials and sheaths.

Selection and process matter together: yarrow in a stag bladder, chamomile in intestine, nettle without an animal sheath, oak bark in a skull, dandelion in bovine mesentery, and valerian as flower extract. The discussion distinguishes making and burying preparations separately from placing the finished substances in manure. It also admits uncertainty about mixing them in a single hole. The editorial notes qualify details, including the oak calcium figure and the exact mesentery intended.

A passage to read closely

We must acknowledge what is legitimate and simply complement it with what can flow from a living conception of the world.

Rudolf Steiner · What Is Biodynamics? · Book chapter 6 · PDF capture page 97.

Read the wording

Read the sentence beside the preceding acknowledgment of practical achievements. “Complement” gives a useful starting principle, although later criticisms of mineral fertilizers and microorganisms are much stronger and must also be acknowledged.

A worked example

A learner says the six preparations are merely six herbs added to a heap. Another includes oak bark, the animal sheaths, seasonal exposure and the distinct extraction of valerian.

The second account better represents the book. Its theory assigns importance to relationships and transformations, so an ingredient list alone misses the proposed method. Accurate description still leaves efficacy to be assessed separately.

Three key takeaways

  • Learn the six preparations as a differentiated system.
  • Material, sheath and process all carry meaning in the text.
  • Read qualifications in the discussion and editorial notes.

Check your understanding

Which preparation has no animal sheath?

Show an explained answer

Nettle, 504, is buried without a bladder or intestine. Valerian, 507, is described separately as a flower extract.

Why are very small additions meaningful within this account?

Show an explained answer

They are claimed to organize processes and relationships in manure rather than supply nutrients in bulk.

Does the 77% statement describe whole oak bark without qualification?

Show an explained answer

No. The editorial note on page 113 says the figure evidently comes from an ash analysis. Omitting that note changes the meaning.
