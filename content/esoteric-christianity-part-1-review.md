## Teaching decision

The speaker is not offering a general history of esoteric Christianity. His organising problem is **how a human bearer could unite primordial innocence and accumulated earthly experience in preparation for Christ**. This determines the course structure. The new English lecture course is separate from the existing bilingual GA 114 book study, with links between them. Ten lessons provide explanations, exact transcript spans, selected readings, source distinctions, analogies, activities, explained answer checks and six notebook fields. The final portfolio brings the first part together. Study estimates total 350 minutes, excluding optional extended reading.

The lessons do not replace the speaker’s interpretation with generic comparative religion. They teach his argument and identify the different status of its components: Gospel narrative, anthroposophical interpretation, attributed but unlocated teaching, explicit personal conjecture, and new course activity. No learner is required to agree in order to demonstrate understanding.

## Bibliographical findings

Four opening sources are identified: GA 114 (15–26 September 1909); three GA 117 lectures (2, 9, 23 November 1909); GA 123 (1–12 September 1910); and GA 148 (October 1913–February 1914). The source guide links the corresponding Rudolf Steiner Archive catalogues and distinguishes a series identification from verification of a particular assertion. No complete fresh reading of all four volumes is claimed.

The publisher identifies **Jon Madsen**, not the transcript’s “John.” The exact edition used in the recording remains unknown. Public Gospel comparison links use another translation. The long Madsen readings are not reproduced in the course.

The cited **Book of the Bee** passage is located in Budge’s Chapter XXXVII and GA 123’s 1965 Appendix I. The appendix numbers the passage XXXVIII. The course uses the chapter title to resolve navigation and distinguishes the medieval witness from the claimed ancient setting. The primary text’s identification of Zârâdôsht with Baruch is retained as a comparison question. See the linked source guide for the full bibliography.

**Robert Powell**, the **Keyserlingk conversation**, **Steiner’s John/Mark lecture references**, the **teacher-reading advice**, the **temple-sleep conception passage**, and the probable **Wolfram von Eschenbach** attribution remain insufficiently specified. ## Fidelity decisions

- Normalise obvious Steiner name variants, Zarathustra, Ahriman, etheric, choleric and “I” where the context is clear. Keep uncertain identities marked as probable. The source register retains the original filename exactly.
- Preserve the speaker’s explicit Mary qualification at **01:43:47–01:44:50**, including his disclaimer of Steiner proof. This is the most consequential attribution boundary in the talk.
- Teach the two-child account as the speaker’s Steiner-based interpretation. Different Gospel details do not independently establish two historical children.
- Correct the categorical statement that Matthew has no angels: Joseph receives angelic dream messages. Avoid attributing the family’s wealth, exactly three kings, an outdoor birth or ox and ass to the Gospel text.
- Present Jesus forty-first/Christ forty-second as the speaker’s interpretation of Matthew’s three groups, not as two separate ancestors expressly listed in Matthew.
- Treat the 5 BCE conjunction date as a source discrepancy against the familiar 7 BCE proposal; original audio is required to decide whether it is a transcription or spoken error. Do not infer a birth date from it.
- Treat the lecturer’s 6000 BCE Zarathustra, Moses chronology, pupil identifications, soul histories, and glosses of Persian names as claims within his account rather than certified history or etymology.
- Correct the Mark/Luke slip contextually in the correspondence map, and flag it in the reference guide. The impossible transcribed John lecture years are not silently converted into guessed dates.
- Preserve the Krishna, Buddha and Grail mentions at the depth actually developed in Part 1. Do not invent additional books or a full comparative curriculum from passing references.
- Luke’s Davidic royal promise is retained despite the innocence/experience contrast. Gospel emphases should not erase details inconvenient to the scheme.
