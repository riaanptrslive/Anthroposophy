**Concept map in prose:** body supplies conditions for experience; soul responds and mediates; spirit receives enduring content and is said to carry individual development. Memory, capacities and deeds connect experience through time. Soul world and spiritland supply the proposed environments of those relations. The path of knowledge aims to transform ordinary faculties into access to those environments. The central explanatory step, repeatedly requiring assessment, is the move from a recognizable experiential distinction to a supersensible ontology.

## 6. Major arguments

1. **Developmental access:** a present limit of perception need not be an absolute limit. The particular spiritual access proposed remains a further claim (TF.06–07; T4.01–02).
2. **Experiential differentiation:** perceiving, feeling and grasping a relation differ. Chapter I develops these into a stronger account of human constitution (T1.01–16).
3. **Capacity and biography:** a life exhibits individual capacities; Steiner argues for an antecedent spiritual history. The argument requires more than the observation that individuals differ (T2.04–09).
4. **Deed and destiny:** actions have effects; Steiner extends that relation across incarnations. Addendum 7 limits the ordinary reasoning to preparation rather than proof (T2.10–14).
5. **Form and formative activity:** a finished shape differs from the activity shaping it; spiritual beings are then proposed as agents (T3.11, 20–22).
6. **Preparation and knowledge:** disciplined thinking, feeling and willing are said to make spiritual perception possible. The text explains their proposed roles while leaving independent validation open (T4.01–12).

The individual chapter argument indexes retain premises, analogies, objections and qualifications. This overview does not replace them.

## 7. Example bank

The meadow and renewed flowers distinguish object, response, remembrance and intelligible relation. Stars illustrate thinking beyond a private impression. The red table distinguishes an experienced quality from an account of its bodily conditions. Oak and crystal distinguish living formation from finished form. Practical technologies illustrate thinking in the service of desires. The pronoun and Jean Paul passage distinguish word from self-awareness. Prism and water separation defend differentiated members within a unity. Rose, twig, literacy and recognition distinguish memory, consequence and capacity. Talent, biography, sleep, migration and cave examples support different parts of the repeated-life argument, with different limits. Painter, architect, blood circulation, weather and ice provide Chapter III's comparisons. City, workshop, child, mathematics and coloured circles support Chapter IV. Every example has its role and limitations explained in the chapter records; a familiar example is never silently promoted into proof of the entire cosmology.

## 8. Interesting-detail bank

The I is not identified by the age at which a child first pronounces “I.” Animal thought is not simply denied. The two sevenfold lists are not identical lists. Memories are not stored unchanged images. The heredity objection is explicitly considered. Ordinary reasoning about reincarnation is qualified as silhouette-like preparation. A later life need not look more accomplished. Ordinary deeds matter independently of fame. The archetypal painter can discover the plan during the work. Spiritual activity can coexist with rest. Natural maternal love complicates a moral ranking of the first aura. Aura colour is not optical colour. Mathematics is optional. Initiation preserves individuality. Research withdrawal is not a permanent rule for life. These details are part of the explanatory coverage, not optional trivia.
